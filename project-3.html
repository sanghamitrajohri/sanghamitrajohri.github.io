BVLOS Drone Search & Rescue Communications

Research Assistant, Project Lead — Control and Trustworthy Robotics Laboratory, University of Washington

Python · Wireshark · UAV Systems

Problem

Search-and-rescue drones operating beyond visual line of sight (BVLOS) lose direct radio contact with the ground station once they're far enough away or blocked by terrain — which caps how far a search area can practically extend before the team loses control and telemetry.

Architecture

What I Built

  • Proposed and led development of a communication system relaying continuous sensor data and control flow between a search drone, a relay drone, and the ground station for BVLOS search-and-locate operations.
  • Led a group of graduate and undergraduate researchers in the Aeronautics & Astronautics department to design and validate the system.
  • Analyzed Wireshark packet captures in Python to evaluate communication quality and reliability under real flight conditions.

Optimization

Data-driven analysis of link quality and dropout patterns from flight-test packet captures informed where the relay needed to hold position, and what telemetry to prioritize when the link degraded.

Evaluation

Results

The relay architecture extended the effective search radius to 1.5× that of existing single-drone methods. The work was peer-reviewed and published:

S. Johri, E. Pollack, E. Madewell, H. Kuni, D. Broyles, J. Vagners, K. Leung, “Beyond Visual Line-of-Sight Uncrewed Aerial Vehicle for Search and Locate Operation,” AIAA SciTech 2024.

Lessons

A relay drone sounds like a purely mechanical fix, but most of the hard problems were about data — deciding what telemetry to prioritize over a lossy, intermittent link, and building the evaluation pipeline to actually prove the relay helped rather than assuming it did.
